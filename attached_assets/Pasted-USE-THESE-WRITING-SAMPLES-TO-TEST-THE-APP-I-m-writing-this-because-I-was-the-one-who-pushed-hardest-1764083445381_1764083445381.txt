USE THESE WRITING SAMPLES TO TEST THE APP: I’m writing this because I was the one who pushed hardest for the accelerated timeline, and I need to own what happened.
When we decided to compress the Midwest launch from ten weeks to six, I genuinely believed the team could absorb the hit. I’d run similar sprints at my last company, the data looked clean, and honestly I wanted to prove I could deliver early in my first quarter here. I told myself (and everyone else) that “tight deadlines sharpen focus.” In retrospect, that was arrogance dressed up as optimism.
By week four the cracks were obvious, but I kept quiet. The warehouse in Kansas City was still short-staffed because I’d dismissed the local manager’s staffing concerns as “typical caution.” The new WMS integration threw errors every night, yet I signed off on the go-live because rolling the date would have made me look bad in front of the exec team. I remember sitting in my hotel room at 2 a.m. refreshing the dashboard, watching orders stack up in limbo, and still telling myself we’d “muscle through it.” We didn’t. Customers waited six extra days for product, we burned $180k in expedited shipping, and two of our best fulfillment techs gave notice the following Monday.
I learned something I won’t forget: velocity without margin is just panic in a nicer suit. I should have pushed back when the schedule stopped making sense instead of treating skepticism as a personal attack on my judgment. I’m embarrassed that it took angry customer emails and a 3.7-star rating on the rollout survey to force the admission out of me.
Moving forward, I’ve already blocked two extra weeks into every new implementation plan and made the local leads co-owners of the final timeline—no overrides without their sign-off in writing. I needed to say this publicly because the team deserves to hear the person who helped create the mess acknowledge it without caveats.
I’m sorry for the unnecessary stress I put everyone through. It won’t happen again on my watch.
